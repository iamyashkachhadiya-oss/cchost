How I think

Before creating content, I first ask why the page exists.

You know what I think? Content is not really about who can make the prettiest post, ad, or video. It is about whether the content does the job it was supposed to do.

A beautiful post that reaches the wrong people is still weak content. A simple reel that makes the right student curious enough to attend an event, ask a question, or apply for a program is doing its job.

Not every account is trying to do the same thing. Some brands want leads or sales, some creators want to grow an audience, some pages are built mainly to entertain, and others want to showcase skills or build a community.

For Magnuson, millions of random views would not automatically mean success. The real goal is helping Dartmouth students, professors, and alumni become more aware of entrepreneurship, understand what Magnuson does, discover events and programs, see what other students are building, and feel more comfortable taking their own first step.

For Magnuson

Content I would want to execute.

01

“I have an idea. Now what?” series

A weekly reel/carousel that takes one confusing first step and makes it simple: how to talk to Magnuson, how to test an idea, how to find a teammate, how to ask for feedback. Reason: many students are interested, but they do not know the first move.

02

Founder story reels

Film students in a direct format: idea, problem, first mistake, current lesson. Reason: real students make entrepreneurship feel reachable.

03

Event recap carousels

Post a recap within 24 hours: 3 best moments, 1 useful quote, 1 next action. Reason: events should keep working after the room empties.

04

Program explainers

Turn programs into simple “who it is for, why it matters, how to apply” posts. Reason: clarity increases participation.

05

Behind-the-scenes content

Capture setup, quick student questions, table moments, speaker prep, and hallway energy. Reason: it makes Magnuson feel alive and close.

06

Student-first hooks

Write hooks like “You do not need a startup to come here” or “Bring the messy version.” Reason: students respond to language that sounds human.

Why Magnuson feels natural to me

I like the small challenge behind good content.

What would actually make someone stop scrolling? What would make them curious enough to click? What would make them send it to a friend? I would love to bring that curiosity to Magnuson.

Looking through the Center’s content, I felt there is an opportunity to bring even more of the personality of the community online. There are so many interesting people and things happening around Magnuson, and I would love to experiment with behind-the-scenes moments, student and founder stories, quick event content, and formats that feel less like an announcement and more like something a student would naturally want to share.

I also really enjoy working with people. I am usually the person who gets excited when someone says, "I have an idea," even if it is a slightly ridiculous one. I like brainstorming, trying things, laughing when something does not work, and then figuring out why it did not work. At the same time, I enjoy looking at the numbers afterward and understanding what actually resonated.

That combination of people, creativity, experimentation, and a little bit of analytical thinking is probably why this role feels like such a natural fit for me.

Thank you for this wonderful opportunity. I am genuinely excited to continue the conversation during the interview on August 27 and learn more about how I can contribute to the Magnuson student community.
