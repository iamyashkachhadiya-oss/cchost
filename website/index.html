How I think

Before creating content, I first ask why the page exists.

You know what I think? Content is not really about who can make the prettiest post, ad, or video. It is about whether the content does the job it was supposed to do.

A beautiful post that reaches the wrong people is still weak content. A simple reel that makes the right student curious enough to attend an event, ask a question, or apply for a program is doing its job.

Not every account is trying to do the same thing. Some brands want leads or sales, some creators want to grow an audience, some pages are built mainly to entertain, and others want to showcase skills or build a community.

For Magnuson, millions of random views would not automatically mean success. The real goal is helping Dartmouth students, professors, and alumni become more aware of entrepreneurship, understand what Magnuson does, discover events and programs, see what other students are building, and feel more comfortable taking their own first step.

My Magnuson thesis

Don’t just build a student Instagram. Build an entrepreneurship community people want to follow.

I see an opportunity to make the Magnuson student account more than an announcement channel. It can become a window into entrepreneurship at Dartmouth: a place where students discover opportunities, meet founders, learn from real entrepreneurial experiences, and see what people are building around them.

01

Relevant attention, not random views

The goal would not simply be more followers or more views. The goal would be to create more relevant attention around entrepreneurship and turn that attention into awareness, participation, community, and action.

02

One account, three audience layers

Dartmouth students come first. Alumni, founders, mentors, faculty, and the broader entrepreneurship community become an amplification layer, not a replacement for the Dartmouth audience.

03

From announcement to media brand

An announcement tells someone what is happening. A story makes someone care that it is happening. I would keep key announcements, but surround them with stories students want to follow.

04

Founder stories

Make the people behind Dartmouth startups visible: what they are building, why they started, the mistake they learned from, how Magnuson helped, and what they would tell other students.

05

Teach without feeling like a classroom

Instead of a plain “what is market validation?” post, ask a Dartmouth founder how they discovered people did or did not want their first idea. The lesson stays; the entry point becomes human.

06

Magnuson behind the scenes

Show what actually happens inside mentorship, Venture Studio, workshops, pitch prep, office hours, and events. Make Magnuson feel like a living community, not just an institutional logo.

07

Opportunities, explained

Don’t just announce a program. Explain the problem, the opportunity, who it is for, how it works, and the next step. The goal is to reduce the distance between interest and action.

08

Community and conversation

Use formats like “Would you invest?”, “What would you build?”, “Founder myth vs. reality,” and “One question, five founders.” The account should talk with students, not only to them.

09

My first 30-day approach

Week 1: understand the account, audience, content, performance, brand voice, and programs. Week 2: build pillars, calendar, workflow, and measurement. Week 3: experiment with formats. Week 4: review what worked, what surprised us, what to repeat, and what to stop.

10

What I would measure

I would measure attention, engagement, and action: reach, watch time, saves, shares, comments, profile visits, website clicks, event registrations, program applications, founder submissions, and student participation. Views are a mechanism, not the mission.

11

The flywheel

Better stories create more relevant reach. More reach gives founders more visibility. More visibility strengthens the brand. A stronger brand brings more stories.

12

My content philosophy

Curiosity earns the first second. Story earns the next thirty. Value earns the follow. Consistency earns the relationship. A clear next step turns attention into action.

Why Magnuson feels natural to me

I like the small challenge behind good content.

What would actually make someone stop scrolling? What would make them curious enough to click? What would make them send it to a friend? I would love to bring that curiosity to Magnuson.

Looking through the Center’s content, I felt there is an opportunity to bring even more of the personality of the community online. There are so many interesting people and things happening around Magnuson, and I would love to experiment with behind-the-scenes moments, student and founder stories, quick event content, and formats that feel less like an announcement and more like something a student would naturally want to share.

I also really enjoy working with people. I am usually the person who gets excited when someone says, "I have an idea," even if it is a slightly ridiculous one. I like brainstorming, trying things, laughing when something does not work, and then figuring out why it did not work. At the same time, I enjoy looking at the numbers afterward and understanding what actually resonated.

That combination of people, creativity, experimentation, and a little bit of analytical thinking is probably why this role feels like such a natural fit for me.

Thank you for this wonderful opportunity. I am genuinely excited to continue the conversation during the interview on August 27 and learn more about how I can contribute to the Magnuson student community.
